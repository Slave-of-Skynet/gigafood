# HTF-01 — Systematic landscape report: sustainable high-temperature retail food packaging

**Project:** Biomentorhub × Profi<br>
**Research cut-off:** 2026-09-26<br>
**Decision scope:** physically available materials, packaging grades, complete constructions and commercial products for hot prepared food, including small/medium portions and whole rotisserie chicken<br>
**Jurisdictions:** European Union, Romania and Moldova<br>
**Companion records:** [source ledger](HTF-01-source-ledger.md) · [rejected-candidate register](HTF-01-rejected-candidates.md) · [supplier RFQ pack](HTF-01-supplier-rfq-pack.md)

> **Mandatory verdict: No fully evidenced candidate found.**

This is not a finding that no useful alternative exists. It is a finding that no candidate in the researched public evidence simultaneously proves the complete strict system: closed transparent pack, ≈250°C exposure, fatty-food contact, leak/barrier performance, the required hold, retail handling, lower-virgin-plastic/sustainability case, and a demonstrated Romania/Moldova end-of-life route.

---

## Status language used throughout

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | Direct evidence covers the exact item/system, condition and claim. |
| **PARTIAL EVIDENCE** | Direct evidence covers only part of the requirement, a platform rather than the exact item, or a narrower condition. |
| **UNKNOWN / NOT VERIFIED** | No adequate public evidence was found. This is not a hidden pass. |
| **FAIL** | Direct evidence contradicts a hard requirement. |
| **COMMERCIAL** | A product/platform is marketed and available; commercial status does not equal qualification for Profi. |
| **PILOT** | Technology/product has testing or launch evidence but scalable supply and exact commercial SKU remain unverified. |
| **R&D** | Material/technology evidence exists without a practical available retail-pack system. |

A melting point, resin-family property, marketing phrase or migration-test condition is never treated as a packaging service-temperature PASS. A tray PASS is not a lid PASS. Recyclable design is not proof of collection, sorting or reprocessing.

# 1. Executive summary

## 1.1 Decision

**No fully evidenced candidate found.** In particular, the search did not identify a commercially evidenced disposable pack that remains closed and transparent at ≈250°C and also has a verified food-contact dossier, six-hour holding performance, bounded cost and practical Romania/Moldova end-of-life route.

The challenge currently contains several different duty cycles. They should not be solved with one unqualified “high-temperature sustainable pack” claim:

1. **Oven preparation near 250°C** — the strictest thermal track.
2. **Rotisserie/hot poultry near 180–190°C** — potentially addressable by specialised oven bags, but not by assuming six hours at that temperature.
3. **Hot-food holding up to six hours** — a separate time/temperature/food-safety requirement, likely around a controlled cabinet temperature rather than oven peak.
4. **Post-cook packing/display** — potentially enables renewable paper/cellulose or removable-lid systems that cannot survive the oven peak.

## 1.2 Strongest findings

- **Sirane Sira-Cook Siralon 21 — COMMERCIAL / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** It is the strongest public whole-poultry cook-in lead: clear nylon, heat-sealable, barrier capable and claimed to 210°C. The exact EU DoC/migration pack, six-hour hold, recycled content and practical EOL route are not public [PRD-01].
- **CRYOVAC Oven Ease — COMMERCIAL / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** The US exact page supports transparency and 204°C for four hours; the UK platform states up to 220°C and poultry uses. The grade, EU compliance package, exposure duration on the UK grade, virgin reduction and local EOL require confirmation [PRD-03, PRD-04].
- **Faerch Evolve CPET — COMMERCIAL / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** CPET is a credible ≤220°C route for portion trays. Exact article C 2227-2AB / 2227022094 publicly reports 69–75% PCR, but no complete exact tray/lid/time-temperature/hot-hold/local-EOL qualification is public [PRD-10]. Historical range claims must not be transferred to unrelated current recipes [PRD-12, INT-02].
- **BIOPAP LC — COMMERCIAL / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** This is the strongest explicit six-hour holding lead: 6 h at 90°C or 5 h at 100°C for the stated LC system, and 185°C/60 min oven evidence. It does not pass 190°C or 250°C and needs exact clear-film/DoC/local-composting qualification [PRD-14–16].
- **Sacma B.Life Gaia — COMMERCIAL / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** A highly relevant renewable rotisserie bag with grass paper, NatureFlex liner/window, grease/liquid resistance and home-compost/paper-recyclability claims. No public numeric maximum temperature/time or exact compliance dossier was found; therefore it cannot be treated as a 190°C PASS [PRD-17–19].
- **Aluminium body + removable or post-applied clear lid — COMMERCIAL / CONDITIONAL.** Aluminium is the strongest ≥250°C body route. A named Aluvin declaration covers listed containers to 350°C for up to 60 min with food-chemistry caveats; Coppice also markets a named tray to 350°C. However, a supplier declaration for common PET lids explicitly requires removal before oven use. This is a two-stage architecture, not a closed transparent oven pack [PRD-23–27].
- **Walki Pack Tray — COMMERCIAL PLATFORM / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** Board constructions are claimed/certified to 220°C/60 min, including a <5% PET grade. A complete clear closure, six-hour hold and named Romania/Moldova paper-mill route are unverified [PRD-20].
- **Thermapet TPET — PILOT / PARTIAL EVIDENCE.** A strategically interesting transparent mono-PET technology to 220°C, but no exact EU commercial SKU, capacity, DoC, price or local recovery route is yet evidenced [PRD-29, PRD-30].

## 1.3 Actionable recommendation

Run **three bounded qualification tracks**, not a single winner announcement:

1. **Whole bird, actual process ≤210°C:** first RFQ Siralon 21 and UK/EU Oven Ease; hold Sacma Gaia as a high-upside renewable comparator pending numeric thermal evidence.
2. **Portions, actual process ≤220°C:** qualify an exact high-PCR CPET article plus exact dual-ovenable clear PET lid, and benchmark Walki Pack Tray where the shape/hold fits.
3. **True ≈250°C:** qualify a named aluminium body with recipe-specific DoC/migration testing and a clear lid applied **after** the oven or removed before it. If clear visibility must remain during the oven step, stop this track: no solution is currently evidenced.

Do not make a winner claim until supplier documents and filled-pack validation close the gates in Section 15.

## 2.2 Critical clarification

The study **does not assume 250°C for six hours**. Peak oven exposure and holding are separate gates. Supplier RFQs must use a time-temperature profile, not a single maximum number.

## 2.3 Strict decision rule

Safety and temperature are non-compensatory. High recycled content, renewable feedstock, lower mass or a compostability certificate cannot offset failure of temperature, migration, leak or hygiene requirements.

# 3. Methodology and evidence hierarchy

## 3.1 Search method

The work used iterative broad and targeted searches until new searches produced only repeated material families, products or unsubstantiated variants. Searches covered:

- high-temperature flexible bags, flow-wrap and lidding films;
- APET, CPET, rPET, tray-rPET, PP and rPP;
- paperboard, pressed/folded board, pulp, bagasse and fibre composites;
- coatings, adhesives, liners, sealants, inks and anti-fog layers;
- aluminium trays and transparent/opaque lidding;
- PLA/CPLA, PHA, starch, cellulose/NatureFlex and other compostables;
- FEP/PFA/PTFE, PEEK, PEI, PPS/PPSU and polyimide;
- glass and reusable systems;
- existing rotisserie, poultry, ready-meal, cook-in and hot-hold products;
- EU/Moldova food-contact law and PPWR/PFAS obligations;
- Romania/Moldova collection, sorting, recycling and composting context;
- public price anchors solely to bound, not prove, cost.

## 3.2 Evidence hierarchy

1. Law/regulation and official authority.
2. Exact product DoC, TDS, independent test or certificate.
3. Exact manufacturer product page.
4. Manufacturer family/platform page.
5. Peer-reviewed/technical paper or recognised industry body.
6. Distributor/trade press.
7. Marketplace/blog — discovery only.

## 3.3 Claim-control rules

- No property was transferred from polymer family to exact packaging grade without marking the gap.
- No tray property was transferred to film/lid, adhesive, ink or label.
- “Ovenable” without a numeric time-temperature condition is **UNKNOWN** for the sponsor gate.
- OM7 migration testing is not a 250°C service claim [REG-03].
- “Recyclable” means design intent unless collection, sorting and reprocessing are separately evidenced.
- EN 13432 does not imply home composting or local acceptance [REG-14].
- Historical Faerch PCR evidence was kept separate from current recipes [PRD-12, INT-02].
- Public list prices were not converted into a fabricated industrial premium.

## 3.4 Research limitation

Supplier-confidential DoCs, migration reports, BOMs, plant capacities and quotes are not public. Absence of public evidence is labelled **UNKNOWN / NOT VERIFIED**, not a definitive technical impossibility. The strict negative verdict concerns the evidence state, not all possible confidential or custom developments.

### Landscape conclusion

There is no material family for which family-level capability alone solves the sponsor need. The strongest routes are application-specific: flexible bag for whole poultry ≤210/220°C, CPET/board for flat portions ≤220°C, BIOPAP for controlled holding/post-cook service, and aluminium for true 250°C with a two-stage visibility architecture.

# 5. Rejected and bounded families

Detailed records are in the [rejected-candidate register](HTF-01-rejected-candidates.md). The principal exclusions are:

1. **APET/rPET display trays:** clear and potentially high-PCR, but a commercial platform is specified only to +70°C [PRD-37]; it is not ovenable at 180–250°C without a specialised process.
2. **PP/rPP:** Faerch’s commercial PP platform is specified up to 121°C [PRD-36], useful for hot-hold/microwave but not conventional-oven 180–250°C duty.
3. **PLA/CPLA/PHA/starch:** heat capability is below the requirement and composting infrastructure is not a given.
4. **Generic bagasse/moulded fibre:** short high-temperature marketing claims do not prove a closed fatty-poultry pack, six-hour hold, particle control or clear window.
5. **Generic paperboard:** requires an exact barrier, glue, ink and clear-film construction; paper alone is not grease/leak proof.
6. **FEP/PFA/PTFE:** either fails the 250°C continuous-use envelope or creates unacceptable PFAS/regulatory/circularity risk, with no appropriate commercial retail pack found.
7. **PEEK/PEI/PPS/PPSU/polyimide:** thermal fragments of a solution, not an affordable transparent retail packaging system.
8. **Glass:** body capability does not prove an ovenable, leakproof, transparent lid; operational burden is high.
9. **Common aluminium + PET dome:** body can pass high heat; clear PET lid must be removed before oven.
10. **Reuse platforms:** existing networks prove logistics, not the required thermal/geometry system or favourable local turn rate.

These are exclusions from the **strict complete-pack gate**, not blanket bans on use in a redesigned process.

## 8.1 Window options

| Window/closure route | Peak evidence | Advantages | Critical failure modes | Current decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole nylon/polyester bag | 210°C Siralon; up to 220°C UK Oven Ease platform | Maximum visibility; very low mass; whole-bird conformity; leak containment | Puncture from bone, seal/vent failure, fogging, multilayer EOL, unknown PCR | First qualification route for ≤210°C whole bird. |

| OvenFlex film | 225°C/2 h film test | Highest numeric transparent-film evidence found below 250°C | Substrate/heat-seal layer may be limiting; laminate EOL; no complete pack | Candidate film for supplier integration, not stand-alone pass. |

| Transparent TPET body/lid | Claimed 220°C | Mono-material visibility and sorting potential | Pilot status, exact lid/DoC/capacity absent | Monitor, not launch candidate. |

## 8.2 Window conclusion

Transparency is the hard incompatibility at 250°C. The aluminium body solves heat but not a clear in-oven lid. Transparent polymers solve display at lower temperatures but no evidenced one reaches the complete 250°C system. Therefore the sponsor must choose one of two process decisions:

- **Window remains through oven:** lower the validated maximum to the exact bag/tray-film capability; or
- **250°C remains:** remove/apply the clear lid outside the oven step.

No wording or sustainability score can eliminate this engineering trade-off.

# 9. Food-contact and packaging regulation

## 9.1 EU baseline

A launch dossier must cover the **full construction** — substrate, PCR layer, coating, liner, sealant, adhesive, ink, anti-fog chemistry, label and closure.

- Regulation 1935/2004 requires no harmful transfer, unacceptable compositional change or organoleptic deterioration, with traceability [REG-01].
- Regulation 2023/2006 requires documented GMP [REG-02].
- Plastic components must comply with Regulation 10/2011 as amended: authorised substances, OML/SML, intended-use conditions and a current DoC [REG-03, REG-06].
- Food-contact recycled plastic must comply with Regulation 2022/1616; “post-consumer recycled” on a website is not enough [REG-04].
- Bisphenol restrictions under Regulation 2024/3190 require exact material review [REG-05].
- PPWR Regulation 2025/40 now applies. For food-contact packaging, Article 5(5) PFAS thresholds apply from 12 August 2026: 25 ppb any targeted PFAS, 250 ppb sum targeted PFAS and 50 ppm all PFAS including polymeric PFAS, with an explanation obligation when total fluorine exceeds 50 mg/kg [REG-07].

**Important:** OM7 or any other migration condition is not evidence of mechanical service at 250°C [REG-03]. Service suitability requires separate physical performance evidence.

## 9.2 Romania

Romania follows the EU framework. Practical launch additionally needs:

- current Romanian language/label and waste-sorting obligations;
- EPR/packaging reporting classification;
- confirmation that supplier DoCs cover the exact market article;
- no inference that RetuRO beverage DRS accepts trays, bags or lid films [EOL-03].

## 9.3 Moldova

At minimum, the local review must cover HG 308/2011, HG 278/2013, HG 492/2015, the FCM GMP regulation HG 594/2014 and applicable sanitary procedures [REG-09–13]. HG 493/2015 is separately listed for ceramic, glass, porcelain, faience, enamelled and glazed articles; it is not the general FCM GMP instrument [REG-12]. A supplier statement “EU 10/2011 compliant” must not be treated as automatic Moldova market authorisation. Moldova Law 306/2018 also makes sanitary-authorisation scope for imported articles a launch question [REG-13]. Obtain:

1. current consolidated Moldovan requirements and required language;
2. confirmation of whether notification, sanitary conclusion or inspection documentation is required;
3. exact acceptance of recycled-plastic food-contact layers;
4. local importer/traceability responsibilities.

## 9.4 Material-specific cautions

- **Aluminium:** there is no single EU-wide plastic-style positive list. Exact alloy, lacquer and intended food/time/temperature matter. The Aluvin declaration warns that food content affects limits and records restrictions for uncoated containers, acidic/salty food and heated aqueous solutions [PRD-23]. Rotisserie juice/salt/seasoning must be validated.
- **Paper/fibre:** qualify additives, wet-strength agents, PFAS, inks, adhesives, optical brighteners and mineral-oil migration. “Paper” is not inherently compliant.
- **Compostables:** certify the exact final printed/converted pack, not just base film. Certificate scope and maximum dimensions/thicknesses matter.
- **Printing/labels:** migration and set-off remain relevant, especially at heat. Require low-migration systems and exact conditions.

# 10. Recycling, composting and local end-of-life reality

## 10.1 Three levels that must not be conflated

1. **Technical recyclability/design:** can a material be reprocessed under a defined protocol?
2. **Collection/sorting acceptance:** will households/stores put it into a stream and will the sorter keep it?
3. **Actual reprocessing at scale:** will a named facility purchase/process the contaminated output into a useful product?

Only all three establish a practical route.

## 10.2 Romania

Official evidence describes a difficult baseline: municipal recycling 12% and landfill 74% in 2022; packaging recycling around 38% in 2021 and 37.3% in 2022 [EOL-01, EOL-02]. Bio-waste treatment capacity is inadequate [EOL-01].

Romania has beverage-PET infrastructure and food-grade rPET production, but:

- RetuRO is for marked beverage containers, not trays or films [EOL-03];
- ALPLA’s documented Romanian capacity is bottle-oriented [EOL-05];
- PET thermoform collection/sorting remains a European development need [EOL-04].

Therefore Faerch/TPET/OSF “recyclable” claims are **not** proof of Romanian tray-to-tray recovery. Require written acceptance from the collector, sorter and reprocessor for the exact tray colour, CPET crystallinity, label, lid film, sealant and residual-food level.

## 10.3 Moldova

UNDP reports plastic recycling averaging about 7% across three years and 3% in 2021, with around 90% of plastic waste landfilled mixed with household waste [EOL-06, EOL-07]. Chișinău has sorting/reprocessing activity and operators list PET, PP, films and paper, but no public evidence confirms routine acceptance of a named greasy hot-food tray/film construction [EOL-08–10].

A Moldova recycling claim must therefore be store/city/operator-specific. At minimum require:

- accepted resin/form/colour/size;
- allowable food contamination;
- collection geography and tonnage;
- sorting technology and bale destination;
- final reprocessed product;
- rejection rate and whether film/lid must be separated.

## 10.4 Compostability

- EN 13432 supports industrial compostability under standard conditions; it does not prove home compostability [REG-14].
- Gaia’s OK Compost Home and BIOPAP/NatureFlex claims are valuable only if the certificate covers the exact final pack and if consumers/operators have a route.
- Romania’s limited bio-waste treatment and Moldova’s mixed-waste landfilling mean “compostable” may still end in landfill.
- Written operator acceptance must include food-soiled packaging, residence time, thickness, labels/inks and whether the facility accepts certified packaging at all.

| Construction | Technical design claim | Romania practical route | Moldova practical route | Current EOL status |

# 11. Cost, mass and sustainability trade-offs

## 11.1 Cost verdict

**The +10–15% premium constraint is UNKNOWN / NOT VERIFIED for every candidate.** No incumbent Profi BOM/cost, annual volume, loss rate or like-for-like delivered supplier quote was available.

Public anchors show only that prices vary widely:

- Faerch distributor listings approximately £0.26–£0.58 per portion tray, lid separate [CST-01].
- BIOPAP retail listing around €0.18–€0.23 for an ambiguously specified tray [CST-02].
- Aluminium poultry example about £0.657 per tray before qualified clear closure [CST-03].
- Conventional US rotisserie bag example about US$0.328 each and not oven-qualified [CST-04].

These differ by market, quantity, geometry, date, VAT, freight and included components. They cannot be divided by an unknown Profi baseline.

## 11.2 Required landed-cost model

For the same product, store and annual volume:

`effective premium = (candidate delivered pack + labour delta + waste delta + line/capex amortisation + EPR fees − recovery value − verified food-waste saving − incumbent delivered pack) / incumbent delivered pack`

Report tray/body, lid/film, label/sleeve, secondary packaging, tooling, equipment, freight, quality losses and disposal/EPR separately. Quote Romania and Moldova separately.

## 11.3 Material trade-offs

| Route | Potential advantage | Potential burden that must be measured |
|---|---|---|
| Thin flexible cook-in bag | Lowest packaging mass; avoids rigid dome/tray; may reduce cooking loss/cleanup | Usually virgin/multilayer and poor recovery; puncture/leak risk; consumer handling |
| High-PCR CPET | Direct virgin-plastic reduction; mature oven tray; exact 69–75% PCR article exists | Opaque/coloured sorting; lid/sealant; local thermoform route; still plastic and heavier than bag |
| Fibre/board | Renewable fibre and potentially very low plastic | Wet/greasy rejection, coatings/adhesives, fibre shedding, mass and transport volume, uncertain local paper route |
| Compostable cellulose | Renewable window/liner and food-soil-compatible end-of-life in theory | No collection/composting may mean landfill; certification/consumer confusion; methane risk in unmanaged landfill |
| Aluminium | True high-temperature body; excellent barrier; high scrap value | Primary aluminium energy/carbon, greater mass, food-specific migration/lacquer, no transparent hot lid, collection loss |
| Reuse | Potentially large per-use reduction at high return/turn counts | Container production, washing energy/water, reverse logistics, loss/breakage, hygiene and customer participation |

No “paper”, “bio-based”, “compostable”, “recycled” or “aluminium” option is automatically sustainable. A comparative LCA or at least ISO-aligned screening using the exact BOM, food-waste effect, grid, transport and end-of-life scenarios is required.

# 12. Strongest 3–5 concepts

## Concept A — Clear whole-bird cook-in bag for a validated ≤210°C profile

**Architecture:** Siralon 21 (primary) or exact UK/EU Oven Ease grade (comparator), heat-sealed/vacuum or supplier-prescribed closure, external paper label/sleeve designed for removal/recycling if possible.

**Why strong:** whole poultry is explicitly named; clear display and low pack mass; commercial filling/sealing formats exist.

**Why it may fail:** actual Profi air/surface peak may exceed grade; bone puncture and steam pressure; six-hour cabinet hold and seal integrity unverified; EU exact dossier and local EOL absent; likely little/no PCR.

**Decision:** first whole-bird RFQ only if sponsor confirms ≤210°C worst-case pack temperature.

## Concept B — Exact high-PCR CPET portion tray + supplier-approved clear ovenable PET lid

**Architecture:** Faerch exact article such as C 2227-2AB (or better-fitting one-compartment exact article) plus exact Sealed Air Sealappeal OSF/TCL grade that both suppliers approve in writing; minimal removable sleeve/label.

**Why strong:** mature commercial portion format; 220°C CPET platform; exact article reports 69–75% PCR; clear anti-fog lid available in the market.

**Why it may fail:** public evidence does not qualify the combination; film may have a lower time-temperature limit; grease on flange, pressure venting and hot peel; 6 h hold unknown; Romania/Moldova tray recovery unproven; whole chicken likely poor fit.

**Decision:** best portion pilot below 220°C after joint supplier sign-off.

## Concept C — Aluminium oven body + clear lid applied after oven

**Architecture:** named aluminium container with verified 250°C profile, recipe-appropriate lacquer if needed, temporary opaque high-heat cover or controlled open roast as process requires, then post-cook clear PET/rPET lid for display/transport.

**Why strong:** only body route with direct public evidence above 250°C; strong grease/moisture/gas barrier; commercial whole-bird/portion formats exist.

**Why it may fail:** additional lidding operation, burn/handling risk, process exposure before lid, clear lid cannot remain in oven, aluminium migration for salty/acidic juices, higher mass/primary-energy burden, microwave instructions and local recovery uncertainty.

**Decision:** leading true-250°C concept only if “window during oven” is not mandatory.

## Concept D — BIOPAP LC Easy for post-cook portioning and controlled six-hour holding

**Architecture:** exact LC/Easy tray and supplier-specified compostable clear film; fill cooked food, seal, hold at validated ≤90°C profile.

**Why strong:** strongest public six-hour evidence; renewable substrate; clear hermetic film and practical semi-automatic sealing system.

**Why it may fail:** not a 190/250°C oven solution; local compost/paper route absent; fibre/delamination/grease performance must be tested; whole chicken formats not shown.

**Decision:** qualify as a separate post-cook hot-hold concept, not as oven replacement.

## Concept E — Sacma Gaia renewable whole-chicken post-cook bag

**Architecture:** exact Gaia grass-paper/NatureFlex bag with transparent window, thermosealed after cooking or used only within supplier-proven reheat/hot-cabinet conditions.

**Why strong:** directly designed for rotisserie/cooked chicken; liquid/grease claims; high renewable content and final-pack EOL certificates claimed.

**Why it may fail:** no public numeric maximum temperature or six-hour profile; certificate scope and local route unverified; paper/film separation and wet-strength at prolonged hold require testing.

**Decision:** high-priority evidence RFQ; do not claim 190°C until the numeric TDS arrives.

# 14. Questions the sponsor must answer

1. Is 250°C an oven-air set point, measured package-surface hotspot or food-core temperature?
2. What is the complete time-temperature curve, including preheat, radiant exposure, broiler/grill, conveyor contact and cooling?
3. Must the consumer-facing transparent window remain attached and closed **inside** the oven, or may it be removed/applied after cooking?
4. Is the chicken cooked in the retail pack, transferred hot into it, or packaged cold for consumer cooking?
5. What is the exact six-hour holding temperature range and relative humidity? Is the pack sealed, vented or repeatedly opened?
6. What are the recipes’ worst-case fat, salt, acid/pH and sauce content?
7. What are the target fill weights, chicken maximum dimensions/protrusion, headspace and number of portion formats?
8. What shelf life is required before/after heating? Is vacuum, MAP, high oxygen, barrier or venting required?
9. Which current filling, tray-sealing, bag-sealing, label and hot-hold equipment must be retained? What line speeds and changeover limits apply?
10. What is the exact current full-pack BOM, component masses, loss rate and landed cost in Romania and Moldova?
11. What annual volumes, store/DC geography, order pattern, lead time and dual-source requirements apply?
12. Is the +10–15% constraint pack purchase price only or total cost including labour, equipment, food waste and EPR?
13. Which sustainability outcome has priority: virgin plastic, absolute plastic mass, carbon, local recovery, renewable content, litter or reuse?
14. Can Profi implement store take-back/segregation, or must the solution use normal household collection?
15. What consumer heating methods must be supported: conventional oven, convection, microwave, air fryer and/or grill?
16. Is a two-stage aluminium body + post-cook clear lid acceptable operationally and in the pitch?

Until these are answered, no supplier can responsibly qualify a product against “250°C plus six hours.”

# 15. Validation and implementation plan

## Stage 0 — Freeze the protocol

Create one signed use-case sheet per format with: actual product, mass/dimensions, pH/salt/fat, fill temperature, oxygen/MAP, seal/vent, time-temperature curve, oven type, hold condition, transport, stack height, consumer use and disposal markets.

**Gate:** no sample testing starts without this profile.

## Stage 1 — Supplier dossier screen

Use the [RFQ pack](HTF-01-supplier-rfq-pack.md). Obtain exact SKU/BOM, TDS, current DoC, OML/SML/NIAS and migration reports, PFAS/total-fluorine statement, GMP, certificates, PCR chain, dimensions/mass, line requirements, capacity and quotes.

**Pass:** every component and condition is mapped; no family-level substitution; supplier gives written intended-use confirmation.

## Stage 2 — Thermal/mechanical bench screen with real filled packs

Test at normal and worst-case oven conditions plus calibrated abuse margin. Record air, tray/film surface, seal and food-core temperatures. Include bone contact and maximum fill.

Measure:

- dimensional change, creep, sag, embrittlement and delamination;
- seal strength/hot tack/peel, vent function and pressure;
- upright, tilt and inversion leak tests;
- puncture and drop performance;
- compression/stacking hot and cooled;
- window haze, fog, distortion, print/label stability and consumer burn risk.

**Pass:** no leak, rupture, deformation that impairs use, unsafe pressure, delamination or visibility failure within pre-agreed tolerances.

## Stage 3 — Food-contact and clean-contact qualification

Use an accredited EU laboratory and exact final printed construction. Apply Regulation 10/2011 conditions where applicable and justify simulants/time-temperature against actual use; test actual worst-case foods where needed.

Include:

- overall and specific migration;
- NIAS risk assessment/screening;
- grease/acid/salt recipe effects;
- organoleptic assessment;
- fibre/particle shedding and surface abrasion after heating/holding;
- set-off/ink/adhesive/anti-fog/coating migration;
- PFAS targeted analysis and total fluorine where relevant;
- aluminium migration and lacquer integrity for the exact poultry juices/seasoning.

**Pass:** legal limits and internal safety limits met with adequate analytical margin; no objectionable odour/taste, particles or fibre transfer.

## Stage 4 — Six-hour hold and food-quality validation

Run the exact cabinet profile independently from peak-oven testing. Sample at 0, 2, 4 and 6 h. Assess seal/leak, condensation, pack strength, window, grease strike-through, fibre softening, food core temperature, microbiological hazard-control validation, yield/moisture, texture and sensory quality.

**Pass:** food-safety plan remains validated and pack/food quality meet specification through six hours. BIOPAP’s 6 h/90°C statement can be a starting point only for the exact LC system.

## Stage 5 — Line and retail trial

Test filling/sealing throughput, changeover, seal contamination, scrap, nesting/denesting, coding, label adhesion, case pack, pallet compression, hot handling, shelf display and customer opening/reclosing.

**Pass:** agreed line rate and quality capability, no new uncontrolled manual step, and store handling is safe.

## Stage 6 — Practical EOL trial

For each market, obtain written acceptance from named collector, sorter and final reprocessor/composter. Supply the exact pack, including food contamination, label, ink and lid. Conduct a bale/plant trial if material volume justifies it.

**Pass:** documented destination, acceptance specification, expected capture/rejection and non-trivial operating scale. A website polymer list alone fails.

## Stage 7 — Cost and environmental comparison

Obtain annual-volume DDP/FCA quotes and measure actual component masses and process deltas. Complete an ISO-aligned screening LCA with Romania/Moldova grid, freight, food waste and realistic EOL scenarios; sensitivity-test landfill and low collection.

**Pass:** total-cost premium is within the agreed interpretation of +10–15%, and the chosen sustainability metric improves without burden shifting that invalidates the claim.

## Stage 8 — Controlled pilot and release

Pilot in representative stores/DCs across both countries. Track leaks, complaints, burns, visibility, sell-through, food waste, returns/sorting and actual cost. Release only after change control, approved artwork/disposal instructions, traceability and supplier quality agreement.

# 16. Pitch-ready claims and prohibited claims

## 16.1 Claims supportable now

Use the qualifier exactly as shown:

- **“A research-saturation landscape review found no fully evidenced disposable retail pack that is simultaneously closed, transparent and qualified at approximately 250°C with demonstrated Romania and Moldova recovery.”**
- **“Commercial transparent cook-in solutions exist for whole poultry at lower temperature envelopes: Siralon 21 is publicly rated to 210°C, while Sealed Air markets a UK Oven Ease platform up to 220°C; exact grades still require qualification.”**
- **“For portion meals, a named Faerch Evolve CPET article publicly reports 69–75% post-consumer recycled content, but a complete tray-plus-lid and local recycling route are not yet verified.”**
- **“BIOPAP LC provides the strongest public hot-holding evidence found: up to six hours at 90°C for the specified system, not at oven peak.”**
- **“Aluminium is the strongest evidenced 250°C body route, but ordinary transparent PET lids must be removed before oven use; visibility therefore requires a two-stage process.”**
- **“The proposal is a gated supplier qualification programme, not an unsupported material substitution.”**

## 16.2 Claims that are not supportable now

Do **not** state:

- “oven-safe to 250°C” for CPET, PET lid, nylon bag, board, bagasse, Gaia or BIOPAP;
- “six hours at 250°C” for any candidate;
- “fully recyclable in Romania/Moldova” without named route evidence;
- “composted locally” from EN 13432 or OK Compost alone;
- “69–75% PCR” for Faerch recipes 6811/7900 based on historical range evidence;
- “zero plastic,” “plastic-free,” “chemical-free,” “non-toxic” or “no migration” without defined methods;
- “more sustainable” from renewable/recycled content alone;
- “within +10–15%” until like-for-like landed quotes and the incumbent baseline exist;
- “EU compliant therefore Moldova compliant”; or
- “250°C capable” based on melting point or OM7.
