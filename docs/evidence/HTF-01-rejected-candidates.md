> “Rejected” below means rejected as a fully qualified answer to the relevant gate. It does not mean the product is defective or unsuitable for every use.

## Disposition codes

- **R-STRICT:** reject for the closed, transparent ≈250°C complete-pack requirement.
- **R-TRACK:** reject for the named bounded track.
- **HOLD:** retain as an RFQ/validation lead.
- **MONITOR:** pilot/R&D lead, not current launch candidate.
- **NO-GO:** evidence shows an irreconcilable hard failure under the current requirement.

## Serious-candidate adversarial summaries

### A. Siralon 21

**Most likely hidden failure:** the supplier’s 210°C statement may depend on cooking time, bag size, venting, lack of direct metal/radiant contact and product moisture. A whole chicken has bones and a large grease load; a successful generic poultry application does not establish Profi’s oven/hot-hold profile.

**Must disprove:** puncture, burst/pressure, seal creep, fogging, ink/label migration, four-to-six-hour integrity and lack of practical recovery.

### B. CRYOVAC Oven Ease

**Most likely hidden failure:** regional pages describe different conditions. The US exact grade is 204°C/4 h and FDA-oriented; the UK platform states 220°C without a public duration. Combining the two would fabricate a 220°C/4 h grade.

**Must disprove:** grade mismatch, EU dossier absence, hold beyond four hours, multilayer EOL and no virgin reduction.

### C. Faerch high-PCR CPET + clear film

**Most likely hidden failure:** the tray may be robust, but the lid sealant, anti-fog, pressure and greasy flange determine the pack. A 69–75% PCR tray can still yield a system with virgin/multilayer film and no local tray outlet.

**Must disprove:** film lower ceiling, contaminated seal failure, peel/sharding, exact recycled-layer compliance, dark/coloured sorting loss and lack of Romania/Moldova reprocessing.

### D. Sacma Gaia

**Most likely hidden failure:** “ovenable” is being interpreted as 190°C without a numeric TDS. Home-compost and paper-recyclability certificates may have exact thickness/dimension/print scopes and may not translate to local disposal.

**Must disprove:** thermal shortfall, prolonged steam/grease weakening, window delamination, fibre transfer, certificate-scope mismatch and landfill outcome.

### E. BIOPAP LC

**Most likely hidden failure:** an attractive six-hour result is applied to the wrong temperature or wrong BIOPAP line. Six hours at 90°C is not six hours at 100°C, 185°C or 250°C.

**Must disprove:** sponsor cabinet hotter than 90°C, closure-film mismatch, wet-rim seal loss, fibre transfer and no compost/paper outlet.

### F. Aluminium two-stage

**Most likely hidden failure:** operational complexity erases the sustainability/cost case. Staff may need to handle very hot trays, change lids, control contamination and prevent clear lids from entering the oven. Salty/acidic poultry juices may require lacquer.

**Must disprove:** migration, lid-change labour, burn risk, loss/leak during transition, microwave misuse, low local recovery and primary-aluminium burden.

### G. Thermapet TPET

**Most likely hidden failure:** strong startup test claims are mistaken for an available certified European retail SKU. Even mono-PET still needs a compatible lid/label and a tray collection route.

**Must disprove:** scale/capacity, EU DoC, exact rPET percentage, forming consistency, thermal ageing, seal compatibility and practical EOL.

## Register conclusion

The negative evidence is consistent across families:

1. Transparent commercial polymers top out below the strict 250°C complete-pack gate.
2. Materials that tolerate 250°C do not provide the required clear, affordable, circular retail closure.
3. Six-hour holding is its own system condition and has only narrow public evidence.
4. Local end-of-life is the least evidenced gate across all otherwise serious candidates.

> **No fully evidenced candidate found.**
